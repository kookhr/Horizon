## [Terence Tao Presents 'Math 2.0' Vision on AI-Transformed Mathematics](https://teorth.github.io/tao-web/slides/math-2-0-caltech-2026.pdf) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Fields Medalist Terence Tao presented slides titled 'Math 2.0' at Caltech, exploring how AI is transforming mathematical research, scientific discovery, and medicine. The presentation describes how, given sufficient compute and access to frontier AI models, one can now obtain solutions to many open problems previously thought difficult in relatively short amounts of time. This represents a paradigm-shifting perspective from one of the world's greatest mathematicians on how AI is fundamentally reshaping mathematical practice and scientific research. The presentation sparked extensive cross-disciplinary debate (307 points, 363 comments), with domain experts in drug discovery and medicine critically engaging with Tao's claims, highlighting both the transformative potential and the limitations of applying AI beyond pure mathematics. Tao describes the 'jagged capability of AI' — where AI can rapidly solve some previously intractable open problems but may struggle with others. He also has an accompanying arXiv paper (2608.16753) titled 'Mathematics in the age of AI' submitted to the Proceedings of the ICM 2026, where he warns that the race for AI-generated answers risks 'flattening' mathematics by prioritizing solutions over the deeper theoretical understanding that pure mathematics seeks to build and maintain.

**Background**: Terence Tao is a Fields Medalist and one of the world's most renowned mathematicians, known for his work across multiple areas of mathematics. He has been an advocate for AI-assisted mathematical research, but has also expressed concerns about its potential to diminish the depth of mathematical understanding. The concept of 'Math 2.0' refers to a new era where AI tools augment or automate aspects of mathematical problem-solving, theorem proving, and scientific discovery, raising questions about the future role of human mathematicians.

**Discussion**: Community discussion was intense and cross-disciplinary. Drug discovery experts pushed back on Tao's cancer medicine slides, noting that many effective medicines (like general anesthesia) lack fully understood mechanisms, making his requirement for human understanding of AI-discovered cures unrealistic. Others highlighted the emotional and professional impact of AI solving open problems — researchers reported having to rewrite grant proposals and feeling as though they'd 'lost an old friend.' One commenter usefully reframed Tao's point in software terms: pure mathematics is about maintaining a high-quality 'codebase' of theory, and open problems are 'lighthouses' illuminating gaps in current understanding, not merely destinations to reach.
