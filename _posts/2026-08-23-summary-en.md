## [Nvidia Pays $6B for Poolside License to Build US Open-Weight AI](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/nvidia-is-spending-6-billion-to-build-a-powerful-u-s-alternative-to-chinese-ai-c51c38cc) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Nvidia has agreed to invest $1 billion in AI startup Poolside at a $12 billion pre-money valuation and pay an additional $6 billion for technology licensing, absorbing over 100 Poolside engineers to work on Nvidia's Nemotron open-weight model project. The deal aims to produce one of the world's most powerful open-weight models, directly competing with Chinese open-source models like DeepSeek and Kimi K3, as well as US closed-source models from OpenAI and Anthropic. This $7 billion deal marks a major strategic shift by Nvidia from primarily supplying AI hardware to becoming a direct participant in the foundation model space, potentially reshaping the competitive dynamics of the global AI landscape. By pursuing a top-tier open-weight model, Nvidia positions itself as a counterweight to both Chinese open-source offerings and American closed-source incumbents, offering a distinct alternative for developers and enterprises. Poolside was founded in early 2023 by Jason Warner, former CTO of GitHub, and Eiso Kant, focusing on building foundation models for software development. Nvidia's Nemotron family already includes open-weight models like the 550-billion-parameter Nemotron 3 Ultra, and the influx of Poolside's engineering talent is expected to accelerate the development of future variants.

**Background**: Open-weight models provide downloadable trained model parameters but may not include the full training data, code, or licensing freedoms required to be considered fully open-source AI. This distinction matters because open-weight models still allow developers to customize and deploy models on their own infrastructure, offering more flexibility than closed-source APIs while keeping some proprietary elements. Nvidia's Nemotron is a family of open-source models with open weights, training data, and recipes designed for building specialized AI agents with reasoning capabilities. The global AI market has seen increasing competition between open-weight approaches, exemplified by China's DeepSeek, and closed-source models from companies like OpenAI.

## [GLM-5.3 Roots Amazon Fire HD Tablet in a Day, Outperforming American AI Models](https://ericpardee.github.io/fire-hd-ownership/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

An author spent $266 on four AI models to attempt rooting an Amazon Fire HD tablet, and Z.ai's GLM-5.3 successfully found unpatched vulnerabilities and created a working exploit within a single day. American AI models refused to assist due to their safety guardrails, while the Chinese-developed GLM-5.3 completed the task. This demonstrates a novel and practical application of AI models for hardware exploitation and reverse engineering, highlighting a growing divergence in how Chinese and American AI labs approach safety guardrails. It also signals that AI is dramatically shrinking the time and expertise needed to find and exploit software vulnerabilities, with significant implications for the cybersecurity industry. GLM-5.3 is Z.ai's latest flagship model, released in August 2026, built on the same base as GLM-5.2 with improvements driven entirely by post-training. The author contrasts the success of GLM-5.3 against American frontier models that were blocked by safety guardrails, though community members noted that simpler manual tools like Fire Toolbox can also debloat and modify Fire tablets without needing AI-driven exploits.

**Background**: Rooting an Android device like the Amazon Fire HD tablet grants users administrative (root) access, allowing them to remove pre-installed bloatware, bypass lockscreen ads, and install custom software. Amazon's Fire OS is a customized version of Android designed to lock users into Amazon's ecosystem, which has historically motivated enthusiasts to find ways to jailbreak or root these devices. AI models are increasingly being used for reverse engineering and vulnerability discovery, as frontier models can analyze decompiled code and identify security flaws faster than manual methods. The divergence in safety approaches between Chinese and American AI labs means that models from different regions may have varying willingness to assist with tasks like exploit development.

## [Ulanqab Becomes China's AI Computing Hub, Chinese Firms Commit 12.5 GW Capacity Surpassing Stargate](https://www.wired.com/story/the-unlikely-place-at-the-center-of-chinas-ai-boom/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A Goldman Sachs report reveals that Ulanqab, Inner Mongolia, has opened or begun construction on nearly 100 data centers since 2016, with Chinese companies committing to a total capacity of 12.5 GW—surpassing the 10 GW scale of OpenAI's Stargate project. Major tech firms including DeepSeek, ByteDance, Alibaba, and Xiaohongshu have built their own AI data centers in the region, with over 70% of the commitments announced in the past year. This development signals that China is rapidly scaling its AI infrastructure to compete globally, with Ulanqab emerging as a critical node in the geopolitical AI race. The concentration of massive data center capacity in a single region highlights both China's strategic push for AI self-sufficiency and the growing environmental trade-offs—water scarcity and coal power reliance—that accompany such rapid expansion. Ulanqab's appeal stems from its cold climate, low electricity prices, and proximity to Beijing, but the region faces serious constraints: annual precipitation is only about 14 inches, and a local water plant was recently forced to shut off supply for 7 hours each night. Additionally, approximately 37% of the region's electricity still comes from coal power, raising concerns about the carbon footprint of China's AI buildout.

**Background**: The Stargate Project is a joint venture announced in January 2025 by OpenAI, SoftBank, Oracle, and investment firm MGX, planning to invest up to $500 billion to build AI infrastructure in the United States by 2029, with an initial $100 billion deployment. The project aims to build approximately 10 GW of data center capacity. Ulanqab, a city in Inner Mongolia with about 1.5 million people, has leveraged its cool climate and low energy costs to attract data center investment since 2016, positioning itself as China's answer to large-scale AI computing hubs.

## [Nvidia Notifies Major Customers of Over 15% AI Server Price Hikes](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-22/nvidia-customers-notified-about-ai-related-price-hikes-above-15) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Nvidia has informed some of its largest customers that prices for AI servers will increase by more than 15%, driven by surging memory chip costs. The price hikes apply to systems shipping in early next year, including those featuring the flagship Vera Rubin and Grace Blackwell chips. This price increase directly impacts major cloud providers like Microsoft, Google, and Oracle, signaling rising costs across the AI infrastructure supply chain. It reflects a shift in bargaining power toward memory manufacturers and could slow AI infrastructure investment or increase costs for end users. The price increases affect systems equipped with Vera Rubin GPUs (manufactured on TSMC's 3nm process with HBM4 memory, scheduled for release in 2026) and Grace Blackwell superchips (combining Blackwell GPUs with ARM-based Grace CPUs via NVLink-C2C interconnect). Manufacturers producing servers for major cloud providers have already notified their customers of the increases.

telegram · zaihuapd · Aug 23, 01:45

**Background**: Vera Rubin is Nvidia's next-generation rack-scale AI platform, named after astrophysicist Vera Rubin, combining Rubin GPUs and Vera CPUs in a tightly integrated architecture. Grace Blackwell is the current-generation platform that pairs Blackwell-architecture GPUs (packing 208 billion transistors) with ARM-based Grace CPUs using 900 GB/s NVLink-C2C interconnects. The memory supply chain is dominated by Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron, whose control over global DRAM production gives them significant pricing power when demand outstrips supply.
